## Problem Context

During the engine boot-up sequence the Tick Controller begins executing interval-driven queues immediately after the runtime and storage infrastructure have been initialized. However, the WebSocket workers responsible for consuming jobs may not have established their connections at this point.

This creates a timing gap between queue execution and worker availability. When the first tick is executed before a worker has registered the scheduler encounters a missing socket consumer connection list and rejects the execution.

In the observed case queue `q_5` initially reported 10 jobs during tick execution followed by a message indicating that no active workers were listening on the queue. The corresponding worker connected shortly afterward and successfully registered itself to `q_5`.

However, worker registration did not immediately restore job execution. Instead the scheduler continued producing empty ticks for several consecutive cycles before normal execution resumed.

## Underlying Cause

The issue appears to originate from the lifecycle of worker-slot registrations maintained in Redis.

During the initial tick execution, the scheduler registers worker slots according to the configured concurrency. Since the socket consumer is not yet available, the dispatch attempt is rejected. However, the worker-slot registrations created during that execution are not necessarily released when this rejection occurs.

Consequently, Redis retains reservations for worker slots that do not have corresponding active consumers.

When the WebSocket worker eventually connects and registers successfully, the scheduler still encounters these previously occupied slots. This prevents the newly connected worker from immediately receiving jobs, even though the network layer correctly identifies it as an active consumer.

The stale registrations remain until they are explicitly cleared or expire through their Redis TTL, which is approximately one minute in the current configuration. During the investigation, manually flushing Redis removed these registrations and normal job execution resumed.

## Queue Execution Context

This behavior is primarily observed with queues configured to use the interval-driven poller. These queues begin their execution cycles as part of the engine's initial scheduling process, making them susceptible to the worker-registration timing gap during startup.

Event-driven queues follow a different execution pattern because their ticks are initiated by incoming events rather than immediately during engine startup. Under normal circumstances, this provides additional time for workers to establish their connections before the first execution occurs.

However, event-driven queues are not inherently exempt from the issue. If an event arrives before the corresponding worker has connected and registered, the same missing-consumer condition and stale worker-slot registration behavior can occur.

## Observed Impact

The primary impact is a temporary loss of job execution capacity despite the presence of successfully connected workers.

The network layer and Redis-backed worker registry can hold conflicting representations of the queue's availability. The network layer considers the worker connected, while the scheduler continues to encounter previously occupied worker slots.

This results in repeated empty tick executions and delayed job processing until the stale registrations are released or expire.

The central problem is the timing dependency between queue execution and worker registration combined with worker-slot reservations that can survive rejected dispatch attempts.
